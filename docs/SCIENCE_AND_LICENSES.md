# Scientific scope and asset provenance

## Original teaching design
All robot meshes, scene arrangements, diagrams, scripts and UI were authored for this project. There are no downloaded third-party humanoid meshes, stock textures or manufacturer marks. The electric humanoid concept contains stereo cameras, an IMU, joint encoders, motors, representative reduction transmissions, controllers, computing, power distribution and battery. It has no specified lifting load, voltage, battery capacity or actuator rating.

The visible materials use standard metallic/roughness shading and scene lights. Reflections are limited to local material lighting, not a captured laboratory HDR environment. The GLB is an interchange snapshot; procedural React geometry is the editable runtime asset. Gear teeth and cabling are explanatory, not engineering drawings. The front panel explodes while the load-bearing frame remains in place. Transparency is an illustrative cutaway, not an X-ray simulation.

## Motion and perception
The elbow demonstrates one axis with a 0–125 degree educational range. The shoulder shows selected axes, not every human shoulder degree of freedom. The fingers are simplified. The walk uses analytic two-link inverse kinematics, planted support foot targets, a small lateral pelvis shift and flat foot orientation. Its center-of-mass dot and support region are **illustrative**, not derived from a mass model. Dynamics, force contacts, collision clearance, torque feasibility and physical stability have not been validated. The scene must not be presented as proof that this machine could walk.

Camera frusta, object boxes, data pulses and label overlays are illustrations. No actual camera feed, object detection network, stereo matcher, state estimator or robot controller runs in this application. A camera records light; perception interprets pixels. An IMU measures acceleration and angular velocity; orientation requires estimation. AI may support recognition or planning, while many low-level motor loops use conventional feedback. A language model is not necessary for every motor command.

The new airframe, deck, battery, radio and bench meshes are independently authored code geometry informed by the existing component photos and published functional dimensions / pin positions. Reference URLs are retained in `src/data/lidar-workshop-layout.ts`: Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1+ / Multi-ranger / Flow V2 / Crazyradio 2.0 pages and mechanical drawings, expansion-pin guidance, and ST’s VL53L1X package specification. The manufacturer’s component-placement PDF and mechanical graphics were inspected as research; they and source supplier CAD are not redistributed in these new meshes. Visual assembly choices are distinguished from published measurements. The centre-ray optical model illustrates geometric intersections and does not predict the real sensor’s full field of view, reflectance or ranging accuracy. The browser host-controller mirror follows the included Python logger’s rule and queue behaviour; that Python logger prints decisions and does not command flight.

The Crazyradio board-and-connector envelope follows Bitcraze’s published 63 × 18 × 8 mm specification; component placements inside that envelope and the illustrative computer are authored visual approximations from the existing kit photograph. The virtual approach uses an authored 80 mm/s forward speed at 150 mm height. It is a separate browser controller, not the supplied Python logger, autopilot code, measured flight dynamics or a prediction of actual flight performance.

The motor shaft / factory-wire reference is Bitcraze’s [7 mm DC motor datasheet](https://www.bitcraze.io/documentation/hardware/motor_7mm/motor_7mm-datasheet.pdf): 7 mm diameter, 16 mm motor length, 3.5 mm shaft length, 0.8 mm shaft diameter and 32 mm wire length. The independent motor routes retain that wire length and avoid the actual flight PCB / can meshes. Propeller diameter, pitch, shaft fit and CW / CCW station pairing follow [Bitcraze’s 47-17 specification](https://www.bitcraze.io/products/propellers-47-17/); the airfoil chord / thickness and hub form are authored visual approximations. The nRF51822 uses the leadless 6 × 6 mm QFN48 package / 0.4 mm contact pitch described in [Nordic’s product specification, mechanical section](https://docs-be.nordicsemi.com/bundle/nRF51-Series/raw/resource/enus/nRF51822_PS_v3.4.pdf). ## Recycled-bottle vacuum workshop references

The new bottle / bulb meshes are independent code geometry informed by the guide’s existing photos: a [behr PET wide-neck bottle example](https://behr-labor.com/en/products/product-details/behroplast_pet_bottles_food_safe_pet_wide_neck_jar_transparent_clear_pet_bottles_with_wide_neck.129.html), [manual siphon bulb / hose example](https://vivfelic.com/products/manual-siphon-pump-kit) and [grommet profile example](https://www.lawsonproducts.com/products/rubber-grommet-3-8-id-52654). Their model dimensions are authored and are not represented as that supplier’s measured CAD. In particular, the marketing pump information does not establish an air-flow curve, cracking pressure, spring constant or exact hose bore. The simulator uses explicit authored values for those quantities. Internal ball-check depictions are teaching cutaways, not a claimed teardown of the pictured product.

The pressure / volume principle follows [NASA’s Boyle’s-law explanation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/boyles-law/). The implemented model conserves ambient-equivalent air between two fixed-temperature cavities and the room through lumped linear flow resistances / ideal one-way elements. It omits distributed hose storage, heat transfer, turbulence, detailed elastomer mechanics and fluid-structure deformation of the bottle. Results will be labelled illustrative; they are not measured pickup predictions. No supplier CAD / research drawings are redistributed.

The paper experiment uses six independently tracked 2.5 × 2.5 × 0.03 mm sheets at an authored density of 800 kg/m³. Relative-air drag uses the quadratic structure of [NASA’s drag equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag-equation/), with an explicitly authored Cd = 1.1 and full sheet area. NASA does not validate this coefficient or this vacuum. The simplified converging nozzle field, contact adhesion, drag-based one-dimensional hose transport, gravity, rapid sheet levelling, inelastic wall contact and resting-sheet friction are qualitative teaching choices. The model omits paper flexibility, orientation-dependent aerodynamics, turbulent wall interactions and two-way coupling of particles into air resistance. It does not predict real pickup performance.

A sheet becomes collected only after traversing the full length-constrained pickup path and crossing its actual end into the collection cavity. Bottle collision uses all eight corners of the same oriented box rendered on screen and the actual inner bottle profile. Resting-sheet contacts use oriented-box separating axes and entry-time ordering, rather than overlaying coincident sheets or incrementing a preset success counter. Six complete cycles require a fully squeezed bulb followed by actual pressure-driven recovery; holding or releasing a partial stroke does not count a full cycle.

The bottle-vacuum assembly jig, grommet folding, tube insertion and cable-tie motions are independently authored instructional geometry. They are not supplier installation tolerances or a validated mechanical assembly. The removed-cap fixture supports its modelled skirt while the bottle is elsewhere; bottle closure holds the lid and both hoses still while turning the lifted bottle at the authored 3.2 mm thread pitch. The nominal 14 mm lid bore, 10 mm tube outside diameter and folding flange dimensions belong to this authored bench; the real guide still requires matching actual tubing / grommets / cap thickness before preparation. The pickup’s slack curve preserves its 700 mm total length during insertion, and the tie’s loop-circumference reduction equals the tail advance. No new supplier CAD or third-party assembly drawing is redistributed.

The completed static workshop displays optional coloured air-direction markers and virtual inspection cutaways. These are explanatory overlays, not visible real air, full CFD streamlines or real holes in the sealed operating bottle. Part callouts follow actual triangle surfaces using barycentric coordinates as meshes deform. Follow-paper close-ups use the same six physical sheet states as the wide view. Optional slow motion changes playback rate; CSV durations are simulation-clock seconds, excluding pauses and nozzle handling. The three-condition records require one complete squeeze / actual elastic recovery at each of six unique marks and derive collected counts from sheets that reached the chamber.

## Virtual water-rocket workshop references (in progress)

The existing lesson begins after an initial push and uses 12 m/s, 9.81 m/s², zero drag and equal launch / return reference heights. The new workshop follows those same equations and its existing `virtual_water_rocket.py` program. [NASA Glenn’s ballistic flight equations](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/ballistic-flight-equations/) support resolving constant horizontal motion and gravity-driven vertical motion. No pressure, water ejection, thrust, aerodynamic stability, finite-body impact or structural strength model is introduced.

The bottle, cap, nose trim, slide keepers, stand, dial and fasteners are independently authored code geometry informed by the application’s existing virtual illustration, not a manufacturer’s real launcher specification or pressure-rated kit. The visible datum is at the reference dot’s initial height, allowing the finite decorative bottle to remain above the field while measurements use a zero-relative-height point model. The eight data / controller tests include executing the project’s actual Python source and matching the three complete ideal launches. Canvas-rendered dial numerals use a system font locally; no remote font or new third-party model / CAD asset is loaded.
